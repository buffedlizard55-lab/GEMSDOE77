<h2>Why hidden faults matter for geothermal</h2>
<ul>
<li><b>DOE GEMS Prize metric:</b> DTI = TP<sub>w</sub> / (TP<sub>w</sub> +0.2 FP<sub>w</sub> +0.8 FN<sub>w</sub>), k(d)=max(1−d/300m,0), α0.2 β0.8. Worked example TP3 FP1.89 FN2→0.60 reproduced in <code>src/gems77/metric.py</code>. <a href="https://www.drivendata.org/competitions/306/competition-doe-gems/page/967/">Problem page</a>.</li>
<li><b>Faulds extensional model:</b> Dilational step-overs, relay ramps, accommodation zones localize permeability under basin fill — blind faults offset basement but no scarp, yet geothermal. <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.geothermics.2011.02.004">Faulds et al. 2010, Geothermics</a>.</li>
<li><b>INGENIOUS compilation:</b> <a href="https://gbcge.org/current-projects/ingenious/">Great Basin Center</a> — source of modern fault inventory in labels.</li>
<li><b>USGS QFaults:</b> <a href="https://www.usgs.gov/natural-hazards/earthquake-hazards/faults">USGS database</a> — basis for competition catalogue (60,988 px in footprint).</li>
</ul>

<h2>Metric insight — why we’re still behind</h2>
<p>Thinning a thick surface while keeping geometry removes already-covered mass, raises credit/pixel to break-even <code>k&gt;α·s</code> (0.052 at 0.26, 0.064 at 0.3195). Group best 0.2600 → public #1 0.3262 needs +25% mean credit/pixel at constant mass. That requires a <b>better detector</b> (not just packing) + scatter-blurred objective (σ=1.85 px, H28 inference). Our H77-C hand-crafted ridge is 12× weaker than a 35-channel GB field (0.019 vs 0.125 holdout) — we document it as negative so next team trains that GB.</p>
