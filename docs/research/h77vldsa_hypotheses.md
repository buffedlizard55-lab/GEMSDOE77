## 1. PhD-Level Forensic Analysis: Why and How `h33-h33-2-b2-20261004T220000Z-e5eb6e7e-zeros` Scored `0.2778` (`OWNER-REPORT`) and How a Submission Can Score Higher

### 1.1 Exact Metric Decision Theory & Kernel-Cover Mechanics
The official DOE GEMS evaluation metric ([DrivenData Competition #306 Problem Description](https://www.drivendata.org/competitions/306/competition-doe-gems/page/967/)) is the Distance-Weighted Tversky Index (`DTI`) with asymmetric false-negative emphasis ($\alpha = 0.2$, $\beta = 0.8$) and linear triangular tolerance kernel $k(d) = \max(1 - d/R, 0)$ of radius $R = 300\text{ m} = 3\text{ px}$ on the `EPSG:32611` 100 m grid (`3730 × 3292`):
$$\text{DTI}(p, G) = \frac{\text{TP}_w}{\text{TP}_w + \alpha\,\text{FP}_w + \beta\,\text{FN}_w}, \quad \text{TP}_w = \sum_{g \in G} \max_{\|x - g\|_2 \le 3} p(x)\,k(\|x - g\|_2), \quad \text{FN}_w = |G| - \text{TP}_w, \quad \text{FP}_w = \sum_{x: p(x)>0} p(x)\Big[1 - \max_{g \in G} k(\|x - g\|_2)\Big].$$
Using $\text{TP}_w + \text{FN}_w = |G|$, the denominator simplifies exactly (`vendor/gems57/metric.py`):
$$D(p, G) = (1 - \beta)\,\text{TP}_w + \alpha\,\text{FP}_w + \beta\,|G| = 0.2\,\big(\text{TP}_w + S - M\big) + 0.8\,|G|,$$
where $S = \sum_x p(x)$ is total emitted prediction mass and $M = \sum_x p(x) \max_{g \in G} k(\|x - g\|_2)$ is self-credit. From this exact functional, three structural theorems govern leaderboard performance:
1. **Why Continuous Probabilistic Heatmaps Collapse (`~0.03–0.09`):** A continuous map with $\bar{p} \approx 0.15$ across $5.106 \times 10^6$ off-catalogue footprint cells emits $S \approx 766{,}000$ units of mass, inflating $0.2\,\text{FP}_w \approx 150{,}000$ in the denominator while $\text{TP}_w \le |G|$ is bounded above by the withheld fault count.
2. **Why Dense 1-px Line Skeletons Underperform Discrete `D2.8` Dots (`0.2333` vs `0.2600` vs `0.2778`):** A single unit dot $p(x)=1$ projects a 29-pixel radial disk $\{y : \|y - x\|_2 < 3\}$. Placing adjacent unit pixels at $100\text{ m}$ ($1\text{ px}$) spacing along a fault ridge pays full $\alpha = 0.2$ false-positive tax on every off-fault dot while adding redundant kernel coverage ($\Delta \text{TP}_w \ll k_{\text{self}}$) on truth cells already covered by neighboring dots (`M > T` in `vendor/gems57/metric.py`). Decimating the ridge into discrete `{0.0, 1.0}` dots spaced by $d_{\min} \approx 2.8\text{ px}$ ($280\text{ m} \approx R$) achieves near-non-overlapping packing of 300 m kernels along strike.
3. **Why Removing the $d_{\text{cat}} \le 2\text{ px}$ ($200\text{ m}$) Catalogue Collar Added `+0.0178` (`0.2600 -> 0.2778`):** In the Initial Prize Round, mapped catalogue pixels (`existing_faults.tif == 1`, `60,988` cells) are masked from evaluation ([DrivenData forum thread 11516](https://community.drivendata.org/t/clarification-on-evaluation-metric-and-existing-faults/11516)). Furthermore, any dot placed at $d_{\text{cat}} = 2.24\text{ px}$ ($\sqrt{5}\text{ px}$) already covers adjacent unmapped branch pixels at $d_{\text{cat}} \in \{1, \sqrt{2}, 2\}\text{ px}$ with kernel weights $k \in [0.33, 0.67]$, whereas dots placed inside $d_{\text{cat}} \le 2\text{ px}$ waste half of their 29-pixel kernel disk on masked catalogue pixels (`labels == 1`).
