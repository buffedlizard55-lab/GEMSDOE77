### 1.3 Exact Distance-Weighted Tversky Index (DTI) Marginal Algebra
Per the official [Problem Description](https://www.drivendata.org/competitions/306/competition-doe-gems/page/967/) and `vendor/gems57/metric.py`, the competition evaluates predictions against unmapped (hidden) faults $G$ (with known catalogue faults masked prior to scoring) using $\alpha = 0.2$, $\beta = 0.8$, and a 300 m ($R = 3\text{ px}$ at 100 m resolution) triangular kernel $k(d) = \max(0, 1 - d/3)$:
$$\text{DTI} = \frac{T}{\alpha\,(T + S - M) + \beta\,K}, \qquad \alpha = 0.2,\; \beta = 0.8$$
where:
- $T = \sum_{g \in G} \max_{x} p(x)\,k(\|x - g\|_2)$ is the total maximum kernel-weighted truth coverage,
- $S = \sum_{x} p(x)$ is the total emitted prediction mass,
- $M = \sum_{x} p(x) \max_{g \in G} k(\|x - g\|_2)$ is the proximity-weighted prediction mass, and
- $K = |G|$ is the number of hidden truth pixels.

For adding a single binary dot ($p(x_0) = 1$, $\Delta S = 1$) at an empty pixel $x_0$ with nearest-truth kernel weight $k = \max_{g \in G} k(\|x_0 - g\|_2)$ and marginal total truth-coverage gain $\Delta T \ge 0$ (which can exceed $k$ when $x_0$'s 3-px disk covers multiple uncovered truth pixels, or be $< k$ when nearby truth pixels are already covered by an existing dot), the denominator changes by $\Delta D = \alpha\,(\Delta T + 1 - k)$, and the exact inclusion condition for improving DTI from current value $s = T/D$ is:
$$\text{new DTI} > s \iff \Delta T > \alpha\,s\,(\Delta T + 1 - k).$$
*(In the special case where a dot covers a single isolated truth cell with no prior coverage, $\Delta T = k$ and the condition simplifies to $k > \alpha\,s$.)*

This exact algebra explains the `H19-5 -> d1.5 -> d2.8 -> H33-2-B2` progression:
1. **Elimination of $\max$-Kernel Redundancy ($\Delta T \approx 0$):** Because $T$ takes a **pointwise $\max$** over predictions within $3\text{ px}$ of each truth pixel $g$, placing a continuous 1-px ridge (`121,131` pixels) puts 5–7 positive pixels inside the 3-px kernel disk of the same fault strand. Those redundant neighbors yield $\Delta T \approx 0$ while still increasing $(S - M)$ when any part of the ribbon wanders off-truth. Thinning to `d = 2.8 px` (`44,090` dots) preserves nearly all of $T$ while cutting $S$ by `63.6%`.
2. **Pruning Sub-Bar Near-Catalogue Halo (`d <= 2 px`):** Known catalogue faults are masked in scoring, and unmapped faults rarely run parallel within `100–200 m` of an already mapped trace (except at branch points). Removing the `6,436` dots within `<= 2 px` of the mapped catalogue cuts low-return mass $S$ with minimal loss of $\Delta T$.

   - **Ceiling A (Greedy Disk Thinning vs. Exact 300 m Submodular Max-Cover):** Isotropic Poisson-disk thinning at `d = 2.8 px` places dots solely by point-to-point exclusion rather than maximizing $\sum_g w(g)\max_{x \in D} k(\|x - g\|_2)$, leaving uncovered triangular gap pockets along `N20°–35°E` Basin-and-Range fault strikes.
