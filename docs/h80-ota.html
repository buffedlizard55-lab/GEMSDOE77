Hypothesis and mechanism

Hypothesis. Wells and springs whose water carries a deep hot-fluid memory — chemical-geothermometer excess ΔT = max(quartz, chalcedony, cation) − surface temperature ≥ 30 °C with max geothermometer ≥ 90 °C (tier A), or measured temperature ≥ 40 °C (tier B) — but which lie more than 1 km from every mapped catalogue fault (“orphans”) are fed by concealed, catalogue-missing faults, and should therefore be collinear along those faults. Mechanism: weighted azimuth-bin collinearity votes between 654 holdout-orphan points (630 vs the full catalogue), splatted ±4 km along each point’s best-supported azimuth, intersected with shipped potential-field ridge support (bands 3/5), as four extra channels on the shared 33-channel stack in a gradient-boosted model. Named non-fault mimic: wells drilled along roads, canals and valley axes, and regional aquifer flow along basin axes. The catalogue is scarp/lineament-led; a blind fault under basin fill can lack both while still heating wells along strike — that is the population this lane adds. It differs from H77D/H77-Step, which used distance halos around thermal points with no collinearity test and no orphan restriction. Ranked candidates: research/hypotheses-h80-2026-10-21.md; preregistered in registry/preregistration_h80.json before Experiment 1. Points: GDR submission 1391 “INGENIOUS Great Basin” well/spring temperature & chemistry (gdr.openei.org/submissions/1391, DOI 10.15121/1881483); potential fields: USGS GeoDAWN; competition/metric: DrivenData 306 problem description.

Why 0.2778 (family champion) — and why this lane does not beat it

The 0.2778 file (GEMSDOE32; the file-to-score link is OWNER-REPORTED — GEMSDOE32’s own page states no organizer score exists for that file) is, measured from its bytes (sha256 c55bafc4…), 37,654 binary dots spaced ~3 px on the H19-5 ridge set with every dot within 200 m of the catalogue deleted: evidence/why_02778.json. Under DTI = T/(0.2(T+S−M)+0.8|G|) each dot must earn more than ≈ α·DTI ≈ 0.056 kernel credit; solid lines waste ~2/3 of their mass on redundant cover, and near-catalogue dots earn ~0 because the organizer scores only unmapped faults. Thinning plus collar-pruning raised credit per dot 0.053 → 0.090 → 0.109 → 0.118 → 0.125 across the lineage (0.1922 → 0.2477 → 0.2600 → 0.2708 → 0.2778, OWNER-REPORTED). H80’s thermal-alignment evidence neither localizes hidden faults at 100 m alone (Exp 1) nor significantly improves a standard model (Exp 2), so it supplies none of the extra credit density needed. The public leaderboard leader was 0.4413 (xiaofanhu, ORGANIZER-CONFIRMED, read 2026-10-21), which at 37,654 dots needs ≈ 0.194 credit/dot — a better detector, not better thinning.
