Why h33 got 0.2778 [OWNER-REPORT] and can we beat it?

Mechanism (measured from archived raster bytes, not an organizer receipt): On 2026-10-21 the public leaderboard showed 0.4413 ORGANIZER-CONFIRMED (#1, xiaofanhu) and 0.2778 ORGANIZER-CONFIRMED (#48, extradr19), without naming the underlying GeoTIFFs. The owner-reported H33 lineage (GEMSDOE32 h33-h33-2-b2-20261004T220000Z-e5eb6e7e-zeros, 37,654 dots) is the H19-5 solid U-Net surface (121,131 px, 0.1922 [OWNER-REPORT]) thinned by Poisson-disk spacing at ≈2.8 px (≈ kernel radius 300 m) to 44,090 dots (0.2600 [OWNER-REPORT]) and then pruned ≤200 m (≤2 px) from the mapped catalogue, removing 6,436 near-catalogue dots (→37,654 dots, 0.2778 [OWNER-REPORT]; see pixel-forensics receipt). Under DTI(α=0.2, β=0.8, R=300 m), DTI = T / (0.2·(T + S − M) + 0.8·K), each unit of emitted mass costs 0.2 in the denominator, so an isolated dot increases DTI iff its marginal kernel credit exceeds 0.2·DTI ≈ 0.0556 (conditional algebra). Spacing dots at ≈300 m eliminates redundant overlapping cover, while near-catalogue pruning removes dots that sit on the shoulder of masked catalogue traces where credit against unmapped faults is near zero.

Why beating 0.2778 (and approaching 0.4413) requires a better field, not better thinning: Emission-side thinning on the H19-5 surface is exhausted; reaching 0.4413 at fixed dot count requires ~60% higher mean kernel credit per dot, which can only come from higher top-of-ranking credit density off-catalogue. See long-form analysis.
