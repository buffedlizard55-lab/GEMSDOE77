### Why the owner's reported ordering is mechanically plausible—but not causally proven

The official metric is a distance-weighted Tversky index with `α=0.2`, `β=0.8`, and a triangular kernel with 300 m support. For a new unit-mass dot, let `q` be its kernel credit to the nearest truth and `ΔT` its *marginal* gain in weighted true-positive credit across all truths. The exact denominator increment is `α(ΔT + 1 − q)`, so the dot improves the current DTI only when `ΔT > α·DTI/(1 − α·DTI) × (1 − q)`. In the simplified one-truth, non-redundant case where `ΔT=q=k`, this reduces to `k > α × DTI`; at `DTI=0.2778`, the break-even `k` is `0.05556`, equivalent to about 283 m under the triangular kernel. Multiple nearby truths, existing coverage, or overlap can change the marginal condition. This is conditional metric algebra—not a score prediction.

The H33 byte audit records 37,654 binary dots, none on the mapped catalogue; `evidence/prior_forensics.json` pins the H33 raster SHA-256 as `c55bafc470054e8271dcb89347a17e07fefe50de6af6e6ba6c4b169ef7ab6fa9`. The repository's forensic narrative records a lineage of progressively thinning one H19-5 surface, followed by removing dots close to the mapped-fault catalogue. That can plausibly raise DTI by reducing redundant prediction mass while retaining kernel-scale coverage. The final byte comparison confirms 6,436 fewer dots from the d2.8 raster to H33 and confirms the removed dots were within 2 px of the catalogue. It does **not** prove that this operation caused the reported leaderboard value: the value is not authenticated to the bytes, and the H33 score changes are owner-reported.

The scientific conclusion is therefore narrow: H33 is a plausible example of *emission-side mass reduction* under this metric, not evidence that further thinning alone will reach the public leader. To beat a good field, the more consequential lever is improving the spatial ranking of off-catalogue fault evidence. The repo's holdouts are local hide-and-recover measurements, not competition scores and not proof of an organizer result.
