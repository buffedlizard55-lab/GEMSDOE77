### Plausible mechanism, and what remains unknown

The pinned shared metric implementation defines pooled distance-weighted Tversky as `DTI = TP / (TP + α·FP + β·FN)`, with `α = 0.2`, `β = 0.8`, and a 300 m triangular kernel. Those constants and the equation describe the shared evaluator implementation; they are **not scores**. A distance-weighted truth contribution saturates where dots are redundant, while predicted mass can continue to incur false-positive cost. For an added dot with marginal truth credit `ΔT` and its self-kernel value `k`, the shared derivation's strict-improvement condition is `ΔT > α·DTI·(ΔT + 1 − k)`. This explains why a local thinning operation can plausibly improve a ratio, but does not show that H33's pruning caused any reported leaderboard outcome. It must be checked on whole withheld fault segments with the shared evaluator, not inferred from raster shape.

The file sequence does show a concrete editing pattern: successive artifacts remove predictions at or near the mapped catalogue, and H33 retains no catalogue-overlapping positive pixels. That could reduce false-positive mass under the stated metric, but the same operation can also remove a true withheld segment near a mapped trace. Without a matched spatial holdout and an organizer receipt, “H33 got the highest score because it pruned redundant dots” is only a mechanism hypothesis—not an established causal explanation.
