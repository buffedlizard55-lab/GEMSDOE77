## Scientific candidate menu for a future separately authorized lane

These are **research ideas, not validated candidates**, and none should be promoted or assigned a submission slot from this review. The exact mechanisms need to be preregistered and tested with the cached-stack hide-and-recover evaluator, single-feature leakage canaries, and registry surface/final-dot audits before implementation affects a candidate artifact.

| Priority | Hypothesis / existing layers | Target signature and rationale | Difference / cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Multi-scale magnetic analytic-signal edges co-located with radiometric K/Th boundaries (`GeoDAWN` magnetic and radiometric bands) | A persistent analytic-signal ridge plus independent compositional boundary could indicate a concealed basement structure continuing beneath basin fill, where mapped surface fault traces are absent. | Tests cross-sensor *edge coincidence*, rather than another topographic scarp or lineament score. Low–medium cost if cached bands and transforms are available. Risks: lithologic contacts and flight-line artifacts. |
| 2 | Curvature break + drainage-network deflection from existing DEM/3DEP derivatives | A fault-controlled channel deflection paired with a linear profile-curvature break could reveal a buried/weakly expressed structure. | Targets geomorphic response rather than pure scarp amplitude; moderate cost. Strong confounders: lithologic transitions, irrigation, and ordinary stream capture. |
| 3 | Geophysical gradient orientation coherence across magnetic and gravity layers | Long-wavelength aligned gradient ridges that persist across scales may indicate a basement discontinuity under cover. | Distinct from surface lidar consensus; medium/high cost, sensitive to survey seams and regional geology. Must avoid using catalogue-derived structure in features. |
| 4 | Thermal/radiometric anomaly gradient aligned with independent structure (`GeoDAWN` radiometric layers; existing thermal/alteration rasters if their provenance is verified) | A spatially coherent K/Th or thermal gradient localized at a structural break could be consistent with hydrothermal alteration on an unmapped conduit. | Multivariate process hypothesis, not simply a higher score on existing fault proxies. Medium cost; weathering, lithology, and survey acquisition can mimic it. |

No external source is required for the first three ideas beyond the already inventoried official GeoDAWN/DEM layers; their actual cached availability must be checked from manifests before viability is asserted. If adding geothermal chemistry, the specific official source is the [GDR INGENIOUS Great Basin compilation, submission 1391](https://gdr.openei.org/submissions/1391); obtainability, license, spatial coverage, and channel semantics must be reverified before use. No hypothesis above is claimed to be novel in the literature or guaranteed to improve DTI.
