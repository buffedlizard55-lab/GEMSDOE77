1. The question this lane answers

The family's best owner-reported submission is h33-h33-2-b2-…-zeros at 0.2778 (OWNER-REPORT; no organizer receipt exists for it), and the public leaderboard top is 0.4413 (leaderboard). Every lane in this repository, including that one, held one thing fixed: the emitter — B = 37,654 binary dots, min_sep = 2.8 px, chosen greedily as the top of a probability surface trained on the mapped catalogue. Every lane then spent its budget on a new surface. H85 asks whether the emitter, not the surface, is the binding constraint.

2. Why the emitter must be the constraint (derivation from the published metric)

The organizer publishes the metric in full (problem description, “Performance metric”; transcribed and unit-tested in vendor/gems57/metric.py):

TP_w = Σ_g max_{x: d(x,g) ≤ R} p(x)·k(d(x,g))      k(d) = max(1 − d/R, 0),  R = 300 m = 3 px
FP_w = Σ_{x: p(x)>0} p(x)·[1 − max_g k(d(x,g))]
FN_w = Σ_g [1 − max_{x: d(x,g) ≤ R} p(x)·k(d(x,g))]
DTI  = TP_w / (TP_w + 0.2·FP_w + 0.8·FN_w)

Three consequences follow directly, and they are the whole lane:

  1. A missed truth cell costs four times an over-prediction (β/α = 0.8/0.2). Under-coverage is the expensive error.
  2. Any prediction inside the kernel radius earns graded credit. The exact marginal test for adding one binary dot with self-credit k and marginal truth credit ΔT is ΔT > α·DTI·(ΔT + 1 − k) (vendor/gems57/metric.py, verified against dti_bruteforce in tests/test_metric.py). At a score of 0.19 a non-redundant dot (ΔT = k) pays for itself whenever k > 0.038, i.e. anywhere within ≈ 2.9 px of a truth cell.
  3. A fixed dot budget therefore caps recall. With 37,654 dots over a 60,988-px catalogue, the family's best cannot cover much more than a third of the network no matter how good the surface is. The published algebra says that is exactly the wrong place to be stuck.

3. The second finding: greedy top-N is absorbed by the catalogue collar

The competition truth is new faults that are not in the USGS/INGENIOUS catalogue by construction (problem description: “faults that are not contained within the current public USGS database … will comprise the test dataset”). But a model trained on the catalogue puts its highest probabilities on the catalogue and in the 1–3 px rasterisation/alignment collar around it. The repository convention allows emission 0.5 px away from a mapped trace (dist_vis > 0.5), so the top of the surface goes straight back into the collar. 4. The surface (deliberately plain)

37 channels derived only from the 19 official training_features.tif bands: robust z-scores, gradient energy, Laplacian and across-strike ridge curvature, 5 px texture, and cross-sensor coupling terms. No catalogue input, no distance-to-mapped-fault channel, no external dataset. That matters: the repository's own IRR-03 and this session's second control both show that a distance-to-visible-fault channel immediately collapses to “train on d = 0” and scores exactly 0.5 AUC on the held-out strands. A label-free surface is the only one that can transfer to a genuinely unmapped fault.

6. What was actually shipped

  • Emission domain: footprint minus a 3.5 px (350 m) collar around every mapped trace.
  • Thinning: 1-px skeleton (3×3 local maxima of the probability surface) before selection.
  • Support: 3.3 × K, the scale-free optimum from the two independent draws. K is anchored at the 60,988 published catalogue cells — the only public count of ground-truth-scale fault cells in this study area — giving 201,260 pixels. A ±2× error in that anchor moves support between ≈100k and ≈400k; the holdout curve is flat over that range (0.153–0.183), which is why the choice is not knife-edge.
  • Values: binary 1.0 on support, 0.0 elsewhere, including outside the footprint (the earlier “Predicted values must be in range [0, 1]” portal error came from non-finite cells; this file has none).

7. The named non-fault process that could mimic this

Plio-Pleistocene Lake Lahontan shoreline benches and fluvial terrace risers produce the identical slope-break/curvature signature as a fault scarp, and 100 m alluvial-fan or basalt-flow contacts add linear property gradients that are not faults. Both survive the label-free surface, and neither is removed by the collar. This is a real source of the false-positive mass in the file and the main scientific risk in it.

8. Limitations — stated, not buried

  • HOLDOUT-DTI is a local spatial holdout over withheld mapped faults, used as a proxy. It is not the organizer's unpublished new-fault truth and it is not a leaderboard score.
  • Support ratio 3.3× is calibrated on withheld catalogue faults; the live new-fault count is unpublished (see §6).
  • The 836-entry remote registry index was not materialised in this session; uniqueness is measured against the 92 rasters present in the checkout, plus the 23 pinned mirrors. Un-fetched entries are a scope limitation, not a claim of absence.
  • No deep model, no 1 m DEM scarp channels, no external datasets. The gain measured here is from the emitter, and the same emitter applied to a stronger surface should add to it.
  • Week-1 promotion is a repository selector decision; the weekly submission slot remains the owner's.
