Pass 3 — original requirements and scientific judgment

What does the reported H33 0.2778 establish?

The user's 0.2778 attribution is OWNER-REPORT, not HOLDOUT-DTI and not an ORGANIZER-CONFIRMED receipt tied to the named GeoTIFF. The pinned H33 mirror is a binary raster with 37,654 positive pixels (local byte-level evidence is linked from the corrected analysis); its SHA pin shows which bytes were examined, not that those bytes obtained that score. The prior pinned source-retrieval receipt records that the GEMSDOE32 owner page itself says no organizer score exists for its artifacts. The family list and leaderboard numbers in the prompt also conflict in date/context; they should not be treated as a fresh official leaderboard observation.

A plausible, unproven mechanism is that sparse, approximately kernel-scale spacing reduces redundant predicted mass while preserving useful neighbourhood coverage under the asymmetric DTI. Pruning around mapped traces may also remove predictions that are poor proxies for hidden faults. Neither mechanism is guaranteed: genuine uncatalogued faults can be near mapped faults, and thinning can remove the only useful coverage for a withheld segment. The exact effect depends on max-kernel coverage over the withheld truth and false-positive mass. The corrected marginal-emission derivation in research/why-h33-scored-02778.md shows why a universal fixed-cost or “optimal thinning” claim does not follow from the score formula.

Therefore, we cannot honestly answer “why that exact file scored 0.2778” from the available evidence, nor claim this branch can beat it. That requires an authenticated artifact-to-score receipt or comparable, leakage-safe holdout results. Holdout fault recovery is also only a proxy for the competition target; it is not direct proof of geothermal-vent discovery.
