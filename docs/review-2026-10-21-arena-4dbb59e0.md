### H33 / score attribution

`research/why-h33-scored-02778.md` and the repository forensic receipt give a plausible *metric mechanism*: the archived H33 raster has 37,654 positive pixels on the H19-5 backbone, and the forensics describe it as the earlier 44,090-dot raster with 6,436 dots at or within 200 m of known catalogue traces removed. Under the repository's DTI setup (α=0.2, β=0.8, 300 m triangular kernel), sparse placement and near-catalogue pruning could reduce redundant prediction mass while retaining useful nearby hidden-fault coverage; they could also delete credit for an unmapped fault near a mapped one. The exact marginal condition is `dT > α·DTI·(dT + 1 − k)`; the simpler `k > α·DTI` applies only when `dT = k`. This is a plausible mechanism, **not causal proof** of why the exact bytes received a particular organizer score. The updated algebra and limitations are documented in `research/why-h33-scored-02778.md`.

The reported `0.2778` file-to-score mapping remains **OWNER-REPORT**, not `ORGANIZER-CONFIRMED`: no authenticated submission-page receipt identifies the raster. The brief and repository contain conflicting score snapshots (including `0.3195`, `0.2778`, and `0.4413`). The archived landing-page text reports a public leaderboard snapshot, but no external page was fetched in this review and a leaderboard row does not identify a repository TIFF.
