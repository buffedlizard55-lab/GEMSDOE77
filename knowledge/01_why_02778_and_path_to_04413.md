## What is confirmed, and what is not

- **ORGANIZER-CONFIRMED (leaderboard row only):** On the repository's dated 2026-10-21 read, the public leaderboard displayed 0.2778 for participant `extradr19` and 0.4413 for first place `xiaofanhu`. The leaderboard row does not identify a TIFF filename, hash, or method.
- **OWNER-REPORT (file-to-score link):** the owner's brief attributes 0.2778 to `h33-h33-2-b2-20261004T220000Z-e5eb6e7e-zeros`. The GEMSDOE32 page itself says no organizer score receipt is archived there. No receipt in this repository binds those bytes to the 0.2778 leaderboard row. Therefore the named-file score must not be described as ORGANIZER-CONFIRMED.
- **MEASURED (raster forensics, not a score receipt):** the indexed H33 TIFF is a single-band float32 binary raster with 37,654 positive pixels. The checked file is spatially associated with the H19-5 support and appears to be a sparse, near-catalogue-pruned dot field. Byte-level properties establish what the raster contains, not which upload earned a leaderboard result.
- **OFFICIAL (metric definition):** DrivenData's problem page defines distance-tolerant scoring with a 300 m triangular kernel and α=0.2, β=0.8. This makes redundant prediction mass costly and gives near-truth predictions credit that decreases with distance. The official page describes the task; it does not certify the historical file-to-score mapping.

## Scientific interpretation — mechanism, not causal proof

For a prediction pixel with realized kernel credit `k`, the exact marginal DTI algebra under the official metric says that adding one unit of prediction mass improves DTI only when `k > α × current DTI` (assuming the score denominator follows the stated Tversky accounting). Thus sparse spacing can reduce redundant false-positive mass, and removing pixels near known catalogue faults can be beneficial if they add little credit for *new* target faults. This is a plausible explanation for why a thinned/pruned raster might rank above a dense line set.

It does **not** prove that these operations caused the account's leaderboard score. It does not demonstrate geothermal-system presence, hidden-fault location, external validity, or that future H19-5 thinning can beat a better detection field. H33's reported performance should be treated as a useful owner-reported hypothesis, not a verified benchmark receipt for these exact bytes.

## Can this project exceed 0.2778?

Possibly, but not demonstrated by the H82 experiment documented in this checkout. Beating a sparse-emission baseline requires better off-catalogue localization (greater hidden-truth kernel credit per unit of emitted mass), not just changing a dot-spacing constant. The H82 local orientation-entropy hypothesis produced **0.097608 HOLDOUT-DTI** (`gems57-pooled-hide-v2`, Seed 20, 11,321 withheld positives, 95% CI `[0.079093, 0.117763]`) and **0.103416 HOLDOUT-DTI** (Seed 30, 11,342 withheld positives, 95% CI `[0.081687, 0.125512]`). These values are below the current local **0.152718 HOLDOUT-DTI** reference (same evaluator, seed 20, 11,321 withheld positives, 95% CI `[0.131548, 0.175185]`). They are local hide-and-recover measurements, not organizer scores, and neither H82 output is approved to submit.

A scientifically credible next step is a new, preregistered field using independent, appropriate data (for example stress-resolved slip/dilation tendencies from the official USGS Great Basin data release) and leakage-controlled spatial validation. That USGS release is public and CC0; it was not downloadable from the sandbox's network allowlist during H82, so it was not used. Candidate rankings, mimic processes, availability checks, and links are in [`research/h82_hypotheses.md`](../research/h82_hypotheses.md).
