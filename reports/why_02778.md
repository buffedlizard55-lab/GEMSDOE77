## Current evidence boundary

- The family attribution of `0.2778` to GEMSDOE32's named H33 TIFF is **OWNER-REPORT**. The archived public leaderboard read records the same value for participant `extradr19` at row #48, but its row does not identify a TIFF or hash. No inspected organizer receipt links the value to the named H33 bytes; the sibling project's page says it has no receipt for that artifact.
- Archived owner-mirror forensics describe a sparse H19-5-derived raster with progressive thinning and mapped-catalogue-distance pruning. This provides a plausible *metric-level* explanation: spacing could reduce redundant prediction mass, and pruning could remove predictions with low expected credit for an off-catalogue target. It does not prove those operations caused the reported score, establish that removed dots were false positives, or identify an optimum.
- For DTI `= T / (α·(T + S − M) + β·K)`, if a proposed dot has nearest-truth kernel weight `k` and marginal weighted-TP gain `dT`, the exact improvement condition (for `α+β=1`) is `dT > α·DTI·(dT + 1 − k)`. Only for a nonredundant dot (`dT=k`) does this reduce to `k > α·DTI`.
- The archived H33 raster's local catalogue hide-and-recover score was **0.004313 HOLDOUT-DTI** (evaluator `gems57-pooled-hide-v2`; 11,342 withheld positives; 95% CI `[0.003508, 0.005188]`; `evidence/holdout_exp1.json`). This measures a local proxy-label task, not public leaderboard performance.
- The old live-score inversion is an **INSTRUMENT** built from reported family scores and archived raster features. Its fit is not ground truth for hidden-fault locations; owner-score attribution, chunk selection, model specification, and spatial dependence limit interpretation. Any model output from that inversion is a projection, not a HOLDOUT-DTI or ORGANIZER-CONFIRMED score.
