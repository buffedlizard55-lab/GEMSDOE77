## 1. Scientific Motivation: The Lateral Outflow Paradox in Blind Geothermal Vent Discovery

More than **75% of geothermal resources in the Great Basin are "blind"** — they lack boiling hot springs or fresh Holocene surface scarps because rapid playa/alluvial-fan sedimentation and steam-heated argillic (smectite) alteration obscure the fault trace at the land surface ([Coolbaugh et al., 2006, *GRC Trans.* 30:867–873](https://www.geothermal-library.org/index.php?mode=pubs&action=view&record=1025144); [Faulds & Hinz, 2015, *World Geothermal Congress*](https://pangea.stanford.edu/ERE/db/WGC/papers/WGC/2015/11066.pdf); [Faulds et al., 2021, *Stanford Geothermal Workshop*](https://pangea.stanford.edu/ERE/db/GeoConf/papers/SGW/2021/Faulds.pdf)).

Every prior hydrothermal feature lane in this repository (`H77D`, `H77-Step`, `H78`) converted point observations from the official **DOE Geothermal Data Repository (GDR) Submission 1391** ([DOI: `10.15121/1881483`](https://gdr.openei.org/submissions/1391)) into raster predictors by applying **isotropic or axis-aligned Gaussian kernels centered at the well or probe coordinate $(r_i, c_i)$**.

From the standpoint of hydrogeology and geothermal reservoir engineering, centering a fault-conduit kernel on a shallow thermal well violates two well-established physical principles:

1. **Topographic Hydraulic-Head Driven Lateral Outflow Plumes ([Ziagos & Blackwell, 1986, *J. Geophys. Res.* 91(B6):6613–6628](https://doi.org/10.1029/JB091iB06p06613); [Richards & Blackwell, 2002, *GRC Trans.* 26:109–114](https://www.geothermal-library.org/index.php?mode=pubs&action=view&record=1020025); [Ferguson et al., 2009, *Geothermics* 38(2):183–194](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.geothermics.2008.09.006)):**
   Buoyant geothermal fluids ascending a steeply dipping blind normal or transtensional fault conduit at a range front or intrabasin step-over encounter the water table / permeable alluvial-fan gravel aquifer at $30\text{–}200\text{ m}$ depth and refract horizontally **down the regional topographic hydraulic head gradient** ($-\nabla Z_{\text{ortho}}$) toward the basin floor for **$0.5\text{ to }3.0\text{ km}$**.
   - Consequently, **drilled agricultural, stock, and TG wells (`well_*` in `gdr_wellspring_in_footprint.csv`) and 2-meter temperature probes (`2m_temperature_probe_n83geo.dbf`)** predominantly sample the **down-gradient lateral outflow tongue** in barren basin fill, **not** the fault upflow conduit itself.
   - Placing probability mass symmetrically around the well shifts fault predictions basinward off the fault trace. Instead, the true fault conduit lies **up-gradient (upslope along $+\nabla Z_{\text{ortho}}$)** at a backtracking distance governed by the fluid's conductive cooling and silica-cation kinetic fractionation, pinned where the upslope trajectory intersects a **buried basement/gravity step, magnetite-destruction trough, or clay-cap breach**.
2. **Orifice vs. Outflow Geochemical & Lithological Discrimination ([Fournier & Truesdell, 1973, *Geochim. Cosmochim. Acta* 37:1255–1275](https://doi.org/10.1016/0016-7037(73)90060-4); [Fournier, 1985, *Rev. Econ. Geol.* 2:45–61](https://pubs.geoscienceworld.org/segweb/books/book/1289/chapter/10702016/The-Behavior-of-Silica-in-Hydrothermal-Solutions); [Ayling et al., 2020, DOE GDR 1391](https://gdr.openei.org/submissions/1391)):**
   - **Natural thermal springs (`spring_*`), silica sinter (`Sinter`), silicified fault breccia/sand (`Silica-cemented sand`, `Silicified fault breccia`), hydrothermal explosion craters (`Explosion craters`), and travertine mounds (`Travertine`)** mark **direct surface or near-surface fault orifices** ($L_{\text{backtrack}} \approx 0\text{ m}$).
   - Conversely, in `Paleo_geothermal_final.csv`, **86 of the 281 in-footprint points (`30.6%`) are labeled `Tufa`**, which in NW Nevada predominantly form as wave-agitated calcium-carbonate towers along Pleistocene Lake Lahontan paleoshorelines ($Z_{\text{ortho}} \le 1,340\text{ m}$) rather than tectonic fault conduits. Prior lanes (`H77-Step`, `H77D`) assigned identical impulse weight `1.0` to shoreline `Tufa` and fault-vent `Sinter`.
   - Furthermore, `gdr_wellspring_in_footprint.csv` provides three distinct chemical geothermometers (`geothermcat_c` $T_{\text{Na-K-Ca}}$, `geothermquartz_c` $T_{\text{quartz}}$, and `geothermchalc_c` $T_{\text{chalc}}$) alongside measured temperature `temp_c` ($T_{\text{meas}}$). Along a lateral outflow plume, silica precipitates out as chalcedony/amorphous silica much faster than Na-K-Ca cations re-equilibrate, creating a diagnostic **Cation–Chalcedony Kinetic Fractionation Gap** ($\Delta T_{\text{cat-chalc}} = \max(0, T_{\text{Na-K-Ca}} - T_{\text{chalc}})$) and **Thermal Dilution Ratio** ($\mathcal{D}_{\text{dil}} = \text{clip}((T_{\text{chem}} - T_{\text{meas}}) / (T_{\text{chem}} - 12^\circ\text{C}), 0, 1)$) that quantify how far downstream the well sits from its parent fault conduit.

## 3. Mathematical Specification of the 16 New `H77V` Feature Channels (`src/gems77v/features.py`)

All 16 channels are strictly **catalogue-free** (zero use of `existing_faults.tif`, `labels.tif`, `gdr_qfaults_traces.csv`, or `dist_known_fault_px`) and **SGMC-free** (zero use of Bands 0 or 1 of `training_features.tif`, which are $1:500\text{k}$ polygon cartoons).

### Block A: Up-Gradient Advection Backtracking & Kinetic Geothermometer Fractionation (`H77V-1`, 6 channels)
Let $Z_{\text{ortho}}(r, c)$ be the 10 m USGS 3DEP orthometric elevation (`topo_10m_derived_100m_u8` Band 9, filled with `det_elev` where needed), smoothed at regional aquifer scale $\sigma_{\text{hyd}} = 8.0\text{ px}$ ($800\text{ m}$). Define the **unit upslope hydraulic backtracking vector field**:
$$\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{\text{up}}(r, c) = \frac{\nabla G_{\sigma=8}(Z_{\text{ortho}})}{\|\nabla G_{\sigma=8}(Z_{\text{ortho}})\| + \varepsilon_z}, \quad \varepsilon_z = 0.5\text{ m/px}.$$

For each observation $i$ in `gdr_wellspring_in_footprint.csv` inside the footprint:
- Measured temperature $T_{\text{meas},i} = \text{temp\_c}$, deep chemical geothermometer $T_{\text{chem},i} = \max(\text{geothermquartz\_c}, \text{geothermchalc\_c}, \text{geothermcat\_c})$.
- **Thermal Dilution Ratio:**
  $$\mathcal{D}_{\text{dil},i} = \text{clip}\!\left(\frac{\max(0, T_{\text{chem},i} - \max(T_{\text{meas},i}, 12))}{\max(T_{\text{chem},i} - 12, 20)}, 0, 1\right).$$
- **Cation–Chalcedony Kinetic Fractionation Index:**
  $$\mathcal{F}_{\text{kin},i} = \text{clip}\!\left(\frac{\max(0, T_{\text{Na-K-Ca},i} - T_{\text{chalc},i})}{80^\circ\text{C}}, 0, 1\right).$$
- **Orifice vs. Outflow Classification:**
  - If `layer` starts with `spring_` (`spring_chemistry`, `spring_temperature`, `spring_features`): natural surface spring orifice ($L_{\text{back}} = 0$).
  - If `layer` starts with `well_`: drilled well sampling an outflow plume. For each backtracking step $s \in \{4, 8, 14\}\text{ px}$ ($400\text{ m}, 800\text{ m}, 1,400\text{ m}$), project the well coordinate upslope along $\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{\text{up}}(r_i, c_i)$ by $\Delta \mathbf{x}_i(s) = s \cdot (0.4 + 0.6\,\mathcal{D}_{\text{dil},i}) \cdot \hat{\mathbf{u}}_{\text{up}}(r_i, c_i)$.

The 6 channels in Block A are:
1. `h77v_00_advect_backtrack_plume`: Multi-step upslope-backtracked thermal/chemical outflow plume density ($s \in \{4, 8, 14\}\text{ px}$, smoothed at $\sigma = 3.5, 6.0\text{ px}$).
2. `h77v_01_advect_ vs_iso_shift`: Differential plume shift ($\text{Backtracked Plume} - \text{Isotropic Unshifted Well Plume}$), highlighting the up-gradient piedmont structural target relative to the down-gradient basin well cluster.
3. `h77v_02_cation_silica_kinetic_backtrack`: Upslope-backtracked Cation–Chalcedony kinetic fractionation field ($\mathcal{F}_{\text{kin},i}$ projected upslope and smoothed at $\sigma = 5.0\text{ px}$).
4. `h77v_03_orifice_spring_sinter_pin`: Zero-offset surface vent orifice field combining natural thermal springs (`spring_*` weighted by $\max(T_{\text{meas}}-15, 0) + 0.5\max(T_{\text{chem}}-60, 0)$) and **tufa-sieved paleo-hydrothermal vents** (`Sinter`, `Silicified fault breccia`, `Explosion craters`, `Silica-cemented sand`, `Travertine`, `Sulfur`, `Alteration` with weight $1.0\text{–}2.0$, while downweighting Pleistocene Lake Lahontan shoreline `Tufa` at $Z_{\text{ortho}} \le 1340\text{ m}$ to $0.15$).
5. `h77v_04_probe2m_upgradient_plume`: Shallow 2-meter temperature probe anomaly ($\max(0, T_{2\text{m}} - \text{median}(T_{2\text{m}}))$) backtracked upslope by $4\text{ px}$ ($400\text{ m}$) along $\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{\text{up}}$ and smoothed at $\sigma = 4.0, 8.0\text{ px}$.
6. `h77v_05_backtrack_step_convergence`: Product of `h77v_00_advect_backtrack_plume` with the local structural step intensity (upslope-realigned basement/gravity gradient + piedmont slope break), isolating where backtracked plumes intersect a physical fault barrier.

### Block B: Critically Stressed Transtensional Dilational Conduit Tensor (`H77V-1`, 4 channels)
Using Band 4 (`geod_2ndinv` $I_2$), Band 7 (`geod_shearrate` $\dot{\gamma}$), and Band 8 (`geod_dilaterate` $\dot{\Delta}$), plus the local dominant topographic/potential-field strike angle $\theta(r, c) \in [0, \pi)$:
7. `h77v_06_transtensional_dilation_ratio`: Volumetric dilatation-to-total strain partitioning $\mathcal{R}_{\text{dil}} = \dot{\Delta} / (\sqrt{\dot{\gamma}^2 + \dot{\Delta}^2} + \varepsilon_{\dot{\varepsilon}}) \times \log(1 + |I_2|)$, separating open dilational/transtensional zones from pure-shear or contractional zones.
8. `h77v_07_crit_stress_dilation_tendency`: Mohr-Coulomb **Dilation Tendency** $T_d(\theta) = \cos^2(\theta - \theta_{\text{opt}})$ (with optimal normal-fault strike $\theta_{\text{opt}} = \text{N}20^\circ\text{E}$ perpendicular to NW Nevada minimum horizontal stress $\sigma_{h,\min} = \text{N}110^\circ\text{E}$) weighted by local structural edge strength and positive dilatation rate.
9. `h77v_08_crit_stress_slip_tendency`: Mohr-Coulomb **Slip Tendency** $T_s(\theta) = |\sin(2(\theta - \theta_{\text{opt}}))|$ weighted by maximum shear strain rate $\dot{\gamma}$ and scarp coherence, capturing conjugate $\text{N}50^\circ\text{E}$ / $\text{N}10^\circ\text{W}$ transtensional relays.
10. `h77v_09_stepover_strain_curvature_gate`: Spatial gradient of dilatation-to-shear partitioning $\|\nabla \mathcal{R}_{\text{dil}}\|_{\sigma=4}$ multiplied by multi-scale topographic/magnetic strike dispersion (capturing fault tips, relay ramps, and releasing bends per Faulds & Hinz, 2015).

### Block C: Steam-Heated Clay-Cap Breach & Hydrothermal Alteration Tri-Pole (`H77V-2`, 3 channels)
11. `h77v_10_claycap_breach_annulus`: Local conductivity gradient $\|\nabla \log(1 + \text{cond\_surf})\|$ multiplied by the regional smectite cap dome elevation ($G_{\sigma=8}(\log(1+\text{cond\_surf})) - G_{\sigma=24}(\log(1+\text{cond\_surf}))$) and suppressed on flat saline playas ($Z_{\text{det}} < -15\text{ m}$ and slope $< 0.8^\circ$).
12. `h77v_11_demag_trough_clay_coupling`: Hydrothermal magnetite-destruction trough ($\max(0, \text{zscore}(\text{TMI\_up150}) - \text{zscore}(\text{RTP}))$ plus positive Laplacian of $-\text{RTP}$ at $\sigma=3\text{ px}$) coupled with `h77v_10_claycap_breach_annulus`.
13. `h77v_12_hydrothermal_tripole_index`: Joint geometric mean / sum of standardized (i) potassic alteration excess ($\text{rank}(K) - \text{rank}(Th)$), (ii) magnetite destruction ($\text{TMI\_up150} - \text{RTP}$), and (iii) clay-cap breach (`h77v_10_claycap_breach_annulus`), gated by proximity to backtracked thermal plumes.

### Block D: Upslope-Realigned Hanging-Wall Gravity Crest & Piedmont Toe Asymmetry (`H77V-3`, 3 channels)
14. `h77v_13_upslope_realigned_grav_base_crest`: Horizontal gradient magnitude of isostatic gravity (`|∇ iso_grav_anom|`) and basement depth (`|∇ depth_to_base_surf|`) **transported upslope along $+\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{\text{up}}$ by $3.5\text{ px}$ ($350\text{ m}$)** to correct for $55^\circ\text{–}60^\circ$ basinward normal-fault dip and align the subsurface density step with the surface fault trace.
15. `h77v_14_piedmont_toe_concavity_grav_lock`: Product of the upslope-realigned gravity/basement crest (`h77v_13`) with the piedmont-toe concave profile curvature (`curv_prof_absmax` from 10 m DEM × asymmetric upslope-minus-downslope relief contrast).
16. `h77v_15_lidar_scarp_polarity_vent_lock`: 1 m LiDAR one-sided face asymmetry ($\max(0, \text{downface\_max} - \text{upface\_max}) \times \text{coh100}$, imputed with 10 m DEM `slope_p90 × aspect_coherence` where 1 m LiDAR is missing `q == 0`) coupled with `h77v_00_advect_backtrack_plume` and `h77v_07_crit_stress_dilation_tendency`.
